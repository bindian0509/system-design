## My Approach to 1:1s

### Structure
- **Frequency**: Weekly, 30-45 minutes, rarely cancelled
- **Ownership**: Their meeting, their agenda
- **Format**:
  - First 15 min: Their topics (blockers, concerns, ideas)
  - Middle: Career/growth discussion or ongoing items
  - Last 5 min: Action items and feedback

### Standing Questions I Ask
1. What's on your mind?
2. Is there anything blocking you?
3. How are you feeling about [current project]?
4. Is there anything I can do differently to support you better?
5. What's one thing you've learned this week?

### Career Development Focus
- **Quarterly**: Deep career conversation (30-60 min)
- **Topics**: Long-term goals, skill gaps, opportunities
- **Output**: Development plan with specific actions

### 1:1 Anti-patterns I Avoid
- Status updates (use stand-ups/async for that)
- Cancelling regularly
- Dominating the conversation
- Avoiding difficult topics

## My Approach to Team Building

### Hiring Philosophy
- **Bar**: Hire people who raise the team's average
- **Focus**: Skills + Culture add (not culture fit)
- **Process**: Structured interviews, consistent rubrics
- **Timeline**: Never rush hiring decisions

### Interview Criteria I Value
1. **Technical competence**: Can they do the job?
2. **Problem-solving**: How do they think?
3. **Collaboration**: Will they make the team better?
4. **Growth potential**: Will they grow with us?
5. **Values alignment**: Do they share our principles?

### Onboarding New Engineers
- 30-60-90 day plan with clear milestones
- Assign an onboarding buddy
- First PR merged in week 1
- Regular check-ins (daily in week 1, then weekly)

## My Approach to Performance Management

### Setting Expectations
- Clear role expectations documented
- OKRs/goals set collaboratively
- Regular feedback (not just at review time)
- No surprises at review time

### Feedback Philosophy
- **Timing**: Immediate (within 24-48 hours)
- **Specificity**: Concrete examples, not vague
- **Balance**: Reinforcing (positive) and redirecting (constructive)
- **Delivery**: Private for critical, public for praise

### Handling Underperformance
1. **Early identification**: Don't wait for it to become serious
2. **Clear conversation**: Specific gaps, expected improvement
3. **Support**: What do they need to improve?
4. **Written plan**: PIP if needed, with clear timeline
5. **Fair exit**: If improvement doesn't happen, part ways respectfully

### Recognizing High Performers
- Public recognition in team meetings
- Nominate for awards/visibility
- Advocate for promotions
- Provide stretch opportunities

## My Approach to Technical Leadership

### Staying Technical
- Code reviews (sampling, not all)
- Architecture discussions
- On-call participation (shadowing)
- Side projects/learning time

### Technical Decision Making
- Team makes most technical decisions
- I weigh in on high-stakes/irreversible decisions
- Prefer RFCs for big changes
- Document decisions and rationale

### Balancing Technical Debt
- Regular tech debt budget (10-20% of sprint)
- Track debt like we track features
- Prioritize debt that slows us down
- Don't let perfection block progress

## My Approach to Cross-functional Collaboration

### Working with Product
- Partner relationship, not order-taker
- Push back when needed
- Contribute to prioritization
- Share technical constraints early

### Working with Other Engineering Teams
- Assume positive intent
- Over-communicate dependencies
- Shared documentation
- Regular sync meetings for interdependent work

### Stakeholder Management
- Regular updates (weekly/bi-weekly)
- Proactive communication on risks
- Manage expectations realistically
- Build relationships before you need them

---

## My Approach to Incidents/Crisis

### During Incidents
- Clear incident commander role
- Focus on resolution, not blame
- Keep stakeholders informed
- Support the team, don't add pressure

### After Incidents
- Blameless postmortem within 48 hours
- Focus on systemic fixes, not individual blame
- Follow through on action items
- Share learnings broadly

## Questions I'm Prepared to Answer

### "What's your management style?"
```
I'd describe my style as "high context, high autonomy." I invest heavily in making
sure my team understands the business context and goals, then trust them to figure
out how to achieve them. I'm available for support but I don't micromanage.
```

### "How do you handle conflict on your team?"
```
I address conflicts early and directly. I start by understanding each person's
perspective privately, then facilitate a conversation focused on shared goals.
Most conflicts stem from miscommunication or different assumptions, not bad intentions.
```

### "How do you prioritize between competing demands?"
```
I use a combination of business impact and urgency. I work with product to
understand what moves the needle most, then factor in technical risk and team
capacity. I'm transparent with stakeholders about trade-offs.
```

### "How do you develop your team members?"
```
I have regular career conversations to understand their goals, then work to
create opportunities aligned with those goals. This might mean stretch assignments,
connecting them with mentors, or advocating for roles that fit their aspirations.
```

## First 90 Days Plan (For New Role)

### Days 1-30: Learn
- Meet everyone on the team 1:1
- Understand current projects and challenges
- Learn the tech stack and architecture
- Build relationships with stakeholders
- Observe, don't change things yet

### Days 31-60: Contribute
- Start adding value in meetings
- Identify quick wins
- Begin building team rituals if missing
- Start having impact on decisions

### Days 61-90: Lead
- Drive a meaningful initiative
- Have perspective on what needs to change
- Build credibility through results
- Set vision for next quarter
