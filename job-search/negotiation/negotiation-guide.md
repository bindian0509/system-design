# Offer Negotiation Strategy Guide

> **Mindset**: Negotiation is expected. Not negotiating leaves money on the table.
> **Goal**: Maximize total compensation while preserving the relationship.

---

## Negotiation Fundamentals

### The Right Mindset
1. **You have leverage** - They've invested time and want you
2. **It's expected** - Recruiters have budget headroom
3. **Be collaborative** - You're solving a problem together
4. **Know your worth** - Research is power

### When NOT to Negotiate
- If you'll accept regardless (still try, but lower stakes)
- If explicitly stated as "final, non-negotiable" (rare)
- Entry-level roles with standardized comp (less flexibility)

## Compensation Components

### Fixed Components
| Component | What It Is | Negotiability |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Base Salary | Monthly/annual fixed pay | High |
| Signing Bonus | One-time payment on joining | Very High |
| Relocation | Moving expenses | High |
| Title | Your designation | Medium |

### Variable Components
| Component | What It Is | Negotiability |
|-----------|------------|---------------|
| Annual Bonus | Performance-based yearly payout | Medium |
| Stock/ESOPs | Equity in company | Very High |
| RSUs | Restricted Stock Units | Very High |
| Retention Bonus | Paid over vesting period | High |

### Other Benefits
| Benefit | Negotiability |
|---------|---------------|
| Start Date | High |
| Remote/Hybrid | Medium-High |
| PTO/Leave | Low-Medium |
| Learning Budget | Medium |
| Equipment | Medium |

## The Negotiation Process

### Phase 1: Pre-Offer Salary Discussion

**If asked "What's your expected compensation?"**

**Option A: Deflect (Recommended)**
> "I'm focused on finding the right role first. I'm confident we can work out compensation if we're both excited about the fit. What's the range budgeted for this role?"

**Option B: Give a Range**
> "Based on my research and experience, I'm targeting ₹X-Y. But I'm flexible for the right opportunity and would love to understand the full package."

**Never:**
- Give a single number (anchors low)
- State current salary as target (often underpaid)
- Say "whatever you think is fair"

### Phase 2: Receiving the Offer

**Do NOT accept immediately, even if it's great.**

**Script:**
> "Thank you so much! I'm really excited about this opportunity. I'd like to take some time to review the full details. When do you need a decision by?"

**Ask for:**
- Full offer in writing
- Breakdown of all components
- Stock details (grant, vesting, strike price if options)
- Benefits summary
- 3-5 business days to decide (minimum)

### Phase 3: The Negotiation Conversation

**Framework:**
1. Express enthusiasm
2. Show you've done research
3. Make your ask
4. Justify with data
5. Be collaborative

**Sample Script:**

> "I'm really excited about this role and joining [Company]. After reviewing the offer and doing some research, I'd like to discuss the compensation.
>
> Based on my experience at Razorpay and the market data I've gathered for similar roles, I was hoping for a base closer to ₹X. I've led teams of [X] engineers, delivered [specific achievements], and have deep expertise in [domain].
>
> Is there flexibility to move closer to that number? I'm also open to discussing other components of the package."

### Phase 4: Counter-Offer Dance

| Scenario | Response |
|----------|----------|
| They say yes to everything | Great! Get it in writing. |
| They counter with partial | Evaluate, negotiate further or accept |
| They say "this is the max" | Try for other components (signing, stock) |
| Hard no on everything | Decide if offer still works for you |

**If they push back:**

> "I understand there may be constraints. Is there flexibility on the signing bonus/stock/other components? I want to make this work for both of us."

## Negotiation Tactics

### 1. The Competing Offer
> "I have another offer at ₹X. I prefer [your company] because of [reasons], but I need to make a financially sound decision. Can we close this gap?"

**Only use if true** - lying damages trust and reputation.

### 2. The Future Value
> "I'm leaving behind ₹X in unvested stock at Razorpay. While I'm excited to join, it would help to offset some of that loss through a signing bonus or additional stock."

### 3. The Scope Increase
> "I noticed the role has broader scope than initially discussed, including [X and Y]. Given this, I believe the compensation should reflect that expanded responsibility."

### 4. The Collaborative Approach
> "I want to make this work. What would you need to see from me to get to ₹X? Is there anything creative we can do with the structure?"

### 5. The Written Follow-up
After verbal negotiation, send email:
> "Thank you for the conversation. To confirm, you mentioned you'll try to get approval for [specific ask]. I'm excited to move forward once we finalize this."

## Common Scenarios

### Scenario: Offer is Lower Than Expected

**Don't:**
- Express disappointment rudely
- Threaten to walk immediately
- Accept defeat without trying

**Do:**
> "Thank you for the offer. I'm excited about the role. However, the base is below what I was expecting based on my research and experience. I was hoping for something closer to ₹X. Is there room to revisit this?"

---

### Scenario: They Can't Move on Base

**Pivot to other components:**

> "I understand base may be constrained. Could we explore:
> - A higher signing bonus to bridge the gap?
> - Additional stock/RSUs?
> - A guaranteed bonus for year one?
> - Early review for salary adjustment in 6 months?"

---

### Scenario: They Say "Final Offer"

**Test if it's really final:**

> "I appreciate you sharing that. Just to be clear, is this final across all components, including signing bonus and stock? I want to make sure I understand the full picture before making my decision."

If truly final:
- Evaluate if it meets your minimum
- If yes, accept gracefully
- If no, politely decline

---

### Scenario: Multiple Offers

**Leverage respectfully:**

> "I'm fortunate to have multiple offers, including one at ₹X. I prefer [your company] because [genuine reasons]. If we can get closer to ₹X, I'm ready to sign immediately."

**Don't:**
- Lie about competing offers
- Create fake urgency
- Play companies against each other aggressively

---

### Scenario: Startup vs Big Tech

**Big Tech:**
- Base usually more negotiable
- Stock refreshers common
- Levels/bands more rigid
- Sign-on bonus flexibility

**Startup:**
- Base may be constrained
- Equity highly negotiable
- Title flexibility
- Perks negotiable

## Negotiation Email Templates

### Initial Counter

```
Subject: Re: [Company] Offer - Compensation Discussion

Hi [Recruiter],

Thank you again for the offer to join [Company] as [Role]. I'm genuinely
excited about the opportunity and the team.

After reviewing the offer and doing market research, I wanted to discuss
the compensation. Given my experience leading teams at Razorpay, my track
record of [specific achievement], and the market rates for similar roles,
I was hoping for a base salary of ₹X and [stock/bonus of Y].

I'm confident in the value I can bring and would love to make this work.
Is there flexibility to move closer to these numbers?

Looking forward to discussing further.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

```

To confirm our discussion:
- Base Salary: ₹X
- Signing Bonus: ₹Y
- Stock: Z RSUs over 4 years
- Start Date: [Date]

Please let me know if I've captured everything correctly. Once I receive
the updated offer letter, I'm ready to sign.

Thanks,
[Your Name]
```

### Accepting the Offer

```
Subject: Re: [Company] Offer - Acceptance

Hi [Recruiter],

I'm thrilled to accept the offer to join [Company] as [Role]. Thank you
for working with me throughout this process.

I've signed the offer letter (attached). Please let me know the next steps
for onboarding.

I'm excited to contribute to the team and hit the ground running.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## Red Flags to Watch For

| Red Flag | What It Might Mean |
|----------|-------------------|
| Extremely aggressive timeline | Pressure tactic or desperation |
| Unwilling to put offer in writing | Sketchy practices |
| Vague about stock details | Could be worthless options |
| Defensive about compensation discussion | Cultural issue |
| Very high variable, low base | Financial instability |

## Negotiation Checklist

### Before Negotiation
- [ ] Research market rates thoroughly
- [ ] Calculate current total comp
- [ ] Define walk-away number
- [ ] Define target number
- [ ] List backup negotiation points
- [ ] Practice talking points out loud

### During Negotiation
- [ ] Stay calm and positive
- [ ] Lead with enthusiasm for role
- [ ] Use data to justify asks
- [ ] Listen actively
- [ ] Take notes
- [ ] Don't accept on the spot

### After Negotiation
- [ ] Get everything in writing
- [ ] Review all details carefully
- [ ] Confirm start date, title, reporting
- [ ] Thank the recruiter
- [ ] Notify other companies professionally

## Final Tips

1. **Be professional** - You might work with these people
2. **Be honest** - Don't fabricate competing offers
3. **Be decisive** - Don't drag negotiations too long
4. **Be grateful** - Thank people for their time
5. **Know when to stop** - Don't nickel and dime after agreement

**Remember**: The best negotiation is one where both sides feel good about the outcome.
